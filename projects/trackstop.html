Overview

Trackstop is an RFID/NFC-based public transport monitoring and passenger information system designed to modernize public bus transportation. The system uses RFID readers installed at fixed checkpoints along bus routes to detect RFID-tagged buses, recording their position in real time and pushing live arrival estimates to a passenger-facing mobile app and digital display boards at bus stops.

Beyond tracking, Trackstop layers an NFC-based digital ticketing system on top — passengers tap an NFC-enabled device on a bus-mounted validator, which verifies the pass with a central server and grants entry, eliminating paper tickets and manual conductor verification entirely. The project was developed at Sai Vidya Institute of Technology and is filed as a patent under The Patents Act, 1970 (Ordinary Application — Patent Office, India).

Key Features

  • RFID checkpoint detection — buses carry RFID tags, readers at fixed stops log every pass
  • Real-time arrival estimation pushed to mobile apps and bus stop display boards
  • NFC-based digital ticket validation — tap-to-verify replaces paper tickets
  • Centralized server for vehicle movement data and passenger flow analytics
  • Reduces manual ticket verification and conductor workload
  • Supports students and commuters via digital passes and NFC-enabled devices
  • Scalable architecture suitable for city-wide transit network deployment
  • Real-time passenger occupancy levels for better travel planning

How It Works

RFID readers are installed at fixed checkpoints along bus routes. Buses carry RFID tags — when a bus passes a checkpoint, the system records its position and updates a central server. The server calculates estimated arrival times for downstream stops and pushes that data to a passenger-facing mobile app and digital display boards at bus stops.

On top of tracking, the system layers NFC-based digital ticketing. Passengers tap their NFC-enabled device on an on-bus validator. The validator contacts the central server, verifies the pass balance or validity, and grants or denies entry — eliminating paper tickets and manual conductor verification entirely.

The three key figures in the patent describe (1) the overall system architecture, (2) the RFID checkpoint detection and information update flow, and (3) the NFC-based digital ticket validation process — together forming a unified real-time transit management platform.

Patent Claims

  1. A public transport monitoring system using RFID-enabled vehicle identification and fixed RFID checkpoint readers for detecting and tracking buses.
  2. A real-time passenger information system configured to estimate and display bus arrival timings based on RFID checkpoint detections.
  3. An NFC-based ticket validation mechanism for digital verification of passenger travel authorization.
  4. A centralized transport data processing system for collecting and managing vehicle movement and passenger information.
  5. A transport management platform designed to reduce manual ticket verification and improve operational efficiency in public transportation systems.

Challenges & Solutions

Challenge: Deployment cost of RFID infrastructure at multiple checkpoints.
Solution: Designed the system to use minimal, strategically placed RFID readers at key route points rather than continuous tracking, reducing hardware requirements without sacrificing location accuracy.

Challenge: Not all passengers own NFC-enabled devices.
Solution: The patent acknowledges this as a known limitation; the system is designed to coexist with conventional ticketing methods during transition, with the digital layer as an opt-in channel.

Challenge: Real-time synchronization between RFID checkpoints and the central server requires stable network connectivity.
Solution: The architecture is built around a centralized server model that can buffer and reconcile checkpoint data even during brief connectivity gaps, maintaining arrival estimate accuracy.

Challenge: RFID detection accuracy can be affected by environmental conditions or tag placement.
Solution: Standardized tag positioning on buses and shielded reader placement at checkpoints were incorporated into the system design to minimize false positives and missed detections.

Results & Impact

Trackstop addresses longstanding inefficiencies in public bus transportation — inaccurate arrival information, manual ticket verification, paper-based ticketing, and poor passenger flow visibility. The system provides transport authorities with a real-time, centralized view of fleet movement, while giving passengers reliable arrival estimates and a seamless tap-to-board ticketing experience.

The project has been accepted and published as a patent (Application No. 202641070548 A) in The Gazette of India: Extraordinary, Part II — validating the invention's novelty and practical contribution to public transportation technology.
